## Principles

1. **Semantic boundaries before scale.** The question is not "how many definitions get ids" but "which facts does Sophia infer correctly, which intermediate objects are worth keeping, which relations hold between representations".
2. **An oracle for every claim.** Each experiment compares Sophia with an independent implementation: metamath.exe, Julia itself, rustc or rust-analyzer, numerical agreement between AD backends.
3. **Specifications, small experiments, benchmarks.** No new major subsystem (SR-126). Where an experiment needs a capability (a Julia process in the loop, IR layers), it states it, and the decision to build it is separate.
4. **Moonkale stays a priority** (SR-125): B-15, the edit-latency benchmark, runs in parallel as soon as Moonkale is on the current revision.

## The experiments, in order

### E-0 Evidence with trust: Metamath proof checking

- **Hypothesis:** SPEC-006 §5 trust records work end to end on a real library: each proof gets `checked by <checker, version>`, and a query can ask "which theorems rest only on checked proofs".
- **Method:** run metamath.exe `verify proof *` (2.85 s for set.mm), record per-proof trust records; optionally a minimal Rust verifier as a second checker.
- **Measures:** time; size of trust records; query time for the trusted closure.
- **Fails if:** trust records cost more than the proofs they annotate, or the trusted-closure query is not expressible in the query language.
- **Teaches:** evidence vs storage ([[Object Categories]] 4), the first real use of trust levels.

### E-1 Julia conformance, parts 3–9

- **Hypothesis:** for each construct (dispatch, world change, macros, closures, mutable globals, parser agreement, include order) Sophia's facts fall into knows / approximates / leaves open, with **no "wrong"** after fixes.
- **Method:** one fixture package per construct plus a Julia script printing the ground truth ([[Julia Conformance]]).
- **Measures:** counts per outcome, per construct, on the fixture and on LinearAlgebra.
- **Fails if:** a "wrong" cannot be fixed or isolated (flagged as unknown).
- **Teaches:** the ledger of what Sophia may claim about Julia.

### E-2 Cross-language structure: Rust `matrixmultiply` vs Julia matmul

- **Hypothesis:** the reference graphs of two independent gemm implementations share recognisable structure (packing, blocking, micro-kernel) that a query can find without names.
- **Method:** ingest `matrixmultiply` (Rust) and Julia's generic matmul (LinearAlgebra `matmul.jl`) or Octavian.jl; compare graph shapes (depth, fan-out, loop nests) by query.
- **Measures:** ingest coverage; graph statistics; whether a structural query matches corresponding parts.
- **Fails if:** nothing beyond trivial shape is shared; that would say structural reuse needs a semantic IR first (L4), which is itself a result.
- **Teaches:** whether cross-language reuse (SR-73) is reachable at L3 or needs L4.

### E-3 Dispatch facts from Julia

- **Hypothesis:** dispatch results recorded from Julia (`which(f, Tuple{…})`, citing a world) are enough to invalidate exactly the affected facts when a method is added.
- **Method:** a fixture with a method table and call signatures; add a method; compare Sophia's stale set (signature overlap) with Julia's invalidations (SnoopCompile's `@snoop_invalidations`).
- **Measures:** precision and recall of the stale set.
- **Fails if:** signature overlap is far coarser than Julia's backedges (many false stales).
- **Teaches:** SP-006 and SR-85 on real dispatch; [[Julia Multiple Dispatch and World Age]].
- **Measured (2026-11-19, B-024):** [[Dispatch Invalidation]]. Not "far coarser" for dispatch facts once a specificity check is added; for compiled code a fact store cannot see what inference relied on, so it marks instances Julia keeps (LinearAlgebra: 13 against 0).

### E-4 Macro expansions as transformation records

- **Hypothesis:** recording `macroexpand` output as a transformation record (input address: macro id, call id, world) makes macro-generated methods visible, closing most of the 1 059 methods Sophia cannot see in LinearAlgebra.
- **Method:** expand LinearAlgebra's module-level macro calls and `@eval` loops in Julia; ingest the expansions as derived programs.
- **Measures:** methods recovered; cost of expansion; size of the expansions.
- **Fails if:** expansions are not reproducible from the recorded inputs (hidden state).
- **Teaches:** derived programs with provenance, on the cheapest possible transformation.
- **Measured so far (E-1 part 5, 2026-11-18):** of the 1 753 methods LinearAlgebra's module-level macro calls make, 1 155 come from 347 `@eval` templates and 97 from macros whose output Sophia does not see at all (`@deprecate`, `@commutative`, `@enum`, constructors). The 1 059 of the hypothesis was counted differently (part 2); expansion would first have to cover the templates, whose inputs are the loop's values.
- **Done (B-025, 2026-11-19), Julia 1.13.1, normaliser pin `06fd112c`.** Julia's side is `tests/julia-semantics/expansions.jl` ([[expansions]]): every `@eval` outside a definition is one record (the template filled from the loop's values, which are recorded); every other module-level macro call is one record (`macroexpand`, one level, hygiene removed); each record's **printed text** is parsed back and evaluated in place of the form. Sophia's side is `src/expansion.rs` ([[expansion]]): the texts spliced into the package in place of their forms and ingested; per expansion a transformation record whose input address covers the macro (its binding; for the package's own macros its definition), the form's source text, the template, the values and Julia's version. Fixture `tests/julia-semantics/expansions/` in CI (`tests/expansions.rs`, 11 tests); LinearAlgebra by `examples/julia_expansions.rs` ([[julia_expansions]]; outputs in `$SOPHIA_CORPORA/e4/`).
  - **LinearAlgebra:** 399 forms expanded (all found in Sophia's parse) into **829 records** (569 `@eval`, 260 macro calls), making **1 458 methods**. Each form's records made exactly the methods the form makes as written (399 of 399, owner by owner, against `macros.jl`). Every record's text parsed back and evaluated (0 failed).
  - **Methods:** 241 known as written (definitions a macro wraps); **1 105 recovered**: a definition with an id in the expansion (`@eval` 1 061, `@commutative` 20, `@deprecate` 18, `@enum` 6); 90 found but refused, all `DependencyRefused` (LAPACK and BLAS wrappers that use what the normaliser already refuses as written, such as `@blasfunc` inside `ccall`); 22 in a definition with an id but no name (`T{P}(…)` heads, part 8's known gap: 14 `@deprecate`d constructors, 8 `AbstractMatrix{T}(…)` from a loop); **0 open**. Of the 1 217 methods not known as written, 91 % are recovered with an id. Whether these are the 1 059 of part 2 was not checked method by method.
  - **Reproducible (the failure condition did not occur):** all 829 records are recomputed from their recorded inputs to the same text (`stable`), two runs in separate processes give identical texts, inputs and methods (timings aside), and no input address is shared by two texts. Hidden state is detected on the fixture: a closure as a loop value (its `repr` does not read back) and a macro with a counter are both `stable = no`; a macro reading a global changed between two identical calls is not (each call re-expands the same in its own state) and shows as one address with two texts (`clashes`).
  - **Costs:** Julia spent 0.35 s making the 829 expansions and 1.6 s parsing and evaluating them (the oracle's whole run, loading LinearAlgebra from source, 19 s); the texts are 699 KB against 432 KB of the forms as written (1.6×); Sophia's two ingests (as written and expanded) take 3.8 s (release build).
  - **What it took:** Julia's printed expansions are not source as they come. Hygiene wrappers print as `$(Expr(Symbol("hygienic-scope"), …))`; gensyms (`var"#40#x"`) depend on the process's history and the shared normaliser refuses them; `@doc` calls inside `@eval` carry absolute file paths in their line comments; `f(x) where T = body` prints as `(f(x) where T) = body`, which the shared crate does not take for a definition (legal Julia, reported to Moonkale). The printer removes hygiene, renames gensyms to plain names, drops macro-call lines and prints definitions in `function … end` form; `$(Expr(:meta, :inline))` stays and is rebuilt when parsed back. Without the template in the address, 70 records shared an address with a different text (several `@eval`s in one loop).
  - **Found on the way:** the expansion's id sees the macro where the source's does not: `@inline inl(x) = x + 1` as written has the id of the bare definition (part 5, wrong), its expansion carries `$(Expr(:meta, :inline))` and gets another id (fixture test). A package's own macros mostly have no id (a macro whose body is a `quote` with `$` is refused), so the address uses the digest of the macro's source text. `@eval M ex` evaluates in module `M`, which the text spliced in place of the form cannot say (`OtherModule`; none in LinearAlgebra).
  - **Reviewed** by Codex ([[Review 2026-11-19 Codex B-025 (external)]]): five findings, all right (a splice evaluated twice, quoted gensyms, an `@eval` inside a macro's expansion spliced twice, a file included twice, state changed between identical calls); each is a fixture case and a test, LinearAlgebra's numbers unchanged.
  - **Not done:** nothing is stored. A place for transformation records in the store is a storage change (SR-126): proposal **B-088**. `@generated` bodies (per signature, at compile time) are not macro expansions and are not covered.

### E-5 Rules vs derived derivatives (ChainRules, ForwardDiff, Zygote)

- **Hypothesis:** id(D(f)) can be computed **before** running D, from (D, version, mode, f's closure, rule world, argument types), and two runs with equal keys give identical derived programs.
- **Method:** small Julia functions using rules and plain code; obtain Zygote's adjoint IR and record its inputs; add an `rrule` and observe which D(g) change.
- **Measures:** whether equal keys give equal outputs; which keys change when a rule is added; transformation vs compile time (separating the two costs E-AD-0 mixed).
- **Fails if:** hidden inputs make D(f) differ under equal keys.
- **Teaches:** input addressing of derived programs; world dependence of transformations ([[AD as a Materialization Case]]).
- **Done (B-026, 2026-11-19), Zygote 0.7.18 on Julia 1.12.7.** Julia's side is `tests/julia-semantics/derivatives.jl` ([[derivatives]]): for each gradient, the `_pullback` instances it reaches (`code_typed` without optimisation; a call Julia cannot infer makes the program `dynamic`, incomplete) and per instance the text of what Zygote runs there: a hand-written adjoint (`@adjoint`) with the method holding its derivative, else the rule `has_chain_rrule` chose with its pullback closure, else its generators' forward and pullback IR (blocks, arguments, branches). It also prints the rule tables (`rrule`, `frule`, `no_rrule`, `no_frule`, `ZygoteRules._pullback`, `ZygoteRules.adjoint`), read from the method table without differentiating anything. Sophia's side is `src/derivative.rs` ([[derivative]]): three keys, each computed without running Zygote, scored against the programs. Fixture `tests/julia-semantics/rules/` ([[Rules]]): one package in ten variants, gradients of 10 functions at `Float64` and `Float32` (220 gradients with the session rows; 198 complete, `dyn`'s are not; 19 503 pairs, across functions too); in CI (`tests/derivatives.rs`, 11 tests); numbers by `cargo run -p sophia-ingest --example julia_derivatives` ([[julia_derivatives]]).
  - **Reproducible:** two runs of all variants in separate processes gave identical programs, rule facts and gradients (timings aside), once the session-numbered closure names (`var"#2849#2850"`) were normalised; closures are told apart by their printed bodies.
  - **Which programs change** (against `base`): a rule for `g` (in the package, by `Zygote.@adjoint`, or from another module) changes D(f1), D(f2), D(g) at both types and nothing else, and two such rules with different pullbacks give different programs; `@non_differentiable sq` changes D(f1), D(f2), D(f3), D(sq); a pirated rule for `Base.sin` at `Float64` changes D(f1), D(f2), D(g) at `Float64` only; a method `g(::Float32)` changes the three at `Float32` only; editing an unused function changes nothing. Equal programs always gave equal gradients, across functions too.
  - **The keys** (each also names the root, f's own methods: `p` and `q` call each other and share a closure):

    | key | what it covers | unsound pairs | missed reuse |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `Store` | D's versions and mode, argument type, ids of f's methods and of its closure in the package (every method of every function used, transitively), the package's own rule definitions for those functions | **113** of 19 503 | 7 |
    | `Rules` | `Store` + every rule method of the session whose signature names a closure function (method-table facts) | **7** | 7 |
    | `Tables` | `Store` + a digest of each whole rule table | **0** | 590 |

  - **So the hypothesis holds with the rule world as method-table facts, and fails with the store alone.** `Store` misses rules made by macros (`@non_differentiable`: Sophia sees a macro call, not the `rrule` methods it makes) and every rule defined outside the package. It gets `Zygote.@adjoint g(x) = …` right by accident: Sophia reads it as a second method of `g` (E-1 part 5's known gap), so `g` turns generic and the ids move. `Rules` is unsound only for the pirated `sin` rule: the closure ends at the package's edge, so a rule for a function outside it is not looked up. `Tables` is sound, but any rule added anywhere re-keys every derivative. The missed reuse of `Store` and `Rules` is `g(::Float32)` re-keying the `Float64` gradients: the closure is per function, not per signature.
  - **Hidden state is in Julia's session, not in the key.** After `rule_g`'s rule is included into a running `base` session, Zygote's generators give exactly the fresh `rule_g` process's programs (equal keys, equal programs), but the gradients the session runs **never call the rule**: 0 calls, against 8 in a fresh process (f1, f2, g, dyn at two types), even for a caller compiled only after the rule was added. Zygote's compiled pullback for `g` is not invalidated by a new `rrule` method (observed on Zygote 0.7.18 and Julia 1.12.7; not checked on 1.13, not reported upstream). A derivative keyed by its inputs and rebuilt from them would be right where the session is stale.
  - **Costs:** Zygote's own calls for every reached instance (dispatch, the rule check, both generators; its decomposition cache emptied first) take 0.004 to 15 ms per gradient; the first gradient call takes 6.7 to 132 ms. Over the 180 complete fresh gradients the transformation is **3.3 %** of the first calls (254 of 7 656 ms; 3.2 % in a second run), 1.6 % on a filled cache. As in E-AD-1, the cost of a first gradient is Julia compiling, not the transformation. The 262 distinct pieces are 213 KB of text.
  - **Reviewed** by Codex ([[Review 2026-11-19 Codex B-026 (external)]]): seven findings, all right (a rule's pullback closure and an `@adjoint`'s derivative were not in the text, no root in the key, branches missing from the IR text, runtime dispatch counted as complete, the timing hit Zygote's cache, versions merged across runs), and an eighth found while fixing them (no root in the program's digest); each is a fixture case or a test.
  - **Not done:** ForwardDiff (no derived program to key: it is f specialised at `Dual`), frules, keyword functions, closures over data, a larger package; programs with runtime dispatch are only marked; the derived programs are not stored (B-088). Proposal **B-090**: extend the `Rules` key to the callees outside the package.

### E-6 One derivative, two layers (Zygote vs Enzyme)

- **Hypothesis:** Sophia can store a derived program at Julia IR (Zygote, Mooncake) and at LLVM IR (Enzyme) and relate them by a `tested`-level equivalence claim on the function they compute.
- **Method:** export both derived programs for a few functions; store as records of two IR languages; record numerical agreement as evidence.
- **Measures:** sizes; agreement; what the store needs to hold LLVM IR.
- **Fails if:** the layers cannot be stored without a new subsystem; then it is a specification result, not code.
- **Teaches:** [[Multi-AST Layering]] on real data.
- **Done (B-027, 2026-11-19), Zygote 0.7.18 and Enzyme 0.13.216 on Julia 1.12.7 (LLVM 18.1.7).** Julia's side is `tests/julia-semantics/layer_programs.jl` ([[layer_programs]]): for E-5's `base` variant ([[Rules]]: f1 to f4, g, sq, h, p, q, dyn at `Float64` and `Float32`), Zygote's program as E-5 prints it (in the same process), Enzyme's reverse-mode module (`enzyme_code_llvm`, the whole module), and both gradients at ten points against a central difference of f in 512-bit BigFloat. Sophia's side is `src/layers.rs` ([[layers]]); in CI as `tests/layers.rs` (17 tests); numbers by `cargo run -p sophia-ingest --example julia_layers` ([[julia_layers]]).
  - **The hypothesis holds without a new subsystem.** For 18 of the 20 gradients (all but `dyn`) both programs are stored, using objects the store already had. Each is a record of its layer's language: `julia-ir` for Zygote's program, which references its piece records, and `llvm-ir` for Enzyme's module. Each has a fact about f (kind `derived-program`, subject f's id, inputs f's closure). One annotation on the pair carries the test as its payload: kind `equivalent-tested`, status `tested`, checker `sophia-e6/numerical`. All of it is written and read back on the four backends, every record verifies, and storing again adds nothing. Queries see it: `annotation(_, "equivalent-tested", p)` gives the 36 programs, `fact(_, "derived-program", f)` the 9 functions. Holding LLVM IR needed nothing new, since a record of another language is bytes with an id. What the store lacks is any reading of those bytes: no parsing or printing, no references from a module to what it calls, no lowering relation.
  - **Agreement:** 18 of 18 pairs tested at 10 points each. The worst Zygote–Enzyme distance is 0.97 units of `eps(T) · max(1, |ref|)` (tolerance 4). Against the 512-bit reference, both are up to 25 units off, and by the same amount: `f2 = sin(x²)·x²` at `Float32` and x = 5.2 is ill-conditioned, so the claim is the two programs' agreement, not their accuracy. `h` has a ChainRules `rrule`. Zygote uses it, and its program is 525 bytes; Enzyme differentiates `exp` itself, in 13.8 KB. The rule worlds differ and the function is the same.
  - **`dyn`** (runtime dispatch): Zygote's program is incomplete, and Enzyme refuses it (`NonInferredActiveReturn`). There is no pair.
  - **LLVM text as Julia prints it is no identity.** A module embeds the addresses of Julia objects: 0 to 11 per module, such as type tags, method instances and the `DomainError` message inside `sin`. It also embeds names numbered by the session (`julia_sin_12951`). After another session history (the rows in reverse order), all 18 raw texts differ. The normaliser reads only literals in pointer positions and replaces each with what it points to; it renumbers session numbers in names. After that, the 18 texts are identical across histories, and every address in the fixture is named. This is a printing convention, not a canonical form: two modules equal up to the names of locals or the order of instructions still differ.
  - **Sizes:** Zygote's 18 programs are 61.8 KB as text and 28.1 KB stored: 23 pieces are shared by 74 references. Enzyme's 18 modules are 243.6 KB, 8.7 times what Zygote's programs take stored (3.9 times their text). A module is mostly what it inlined or carries: 35 KB where `sin` is reached (f1, f2, g at `Float64`) against 2.7 KB for `sq`. Split into function definitions, the modules hold 41 functions, 29 distinct by text (171 KB, of which 77 KB distinct), plus 72.6 KB of headers, declarations and attributes. An SQLite store with the package grows from 119 KB to 553 KB with both layers.
  - **Times:** Enzyme's first `autodiff` takes 22 to 888 ms per gradient (f1, the first, includes compiling Enzyme's own code), and exporting the module again takes 0.12 to 3.5 s. Zygote's first gradient takes 10 to 134 ms.
  - **Loading Enzyme leaves Zygote's programs as E-5 found them.** The `derived`, `instance` and `rule` rows are identical to `rules/truth.tsv`. But Enzyme adds one `rrule` and one `frule` (`ignore_derivatives`, from EnzymeCore's ChainRulesCore extension). E-5's `Tables` key would re-key every derivative; its `Rules` key, none. This is a real case of `Tables`' imprecision.
  - **Reviewed** by Codex ([[Review 2026-11-19 Codex B-027 (external)]]): eleven findings, ten right and one partly right. Five concern the normaliser: a number in the address range read as a pointer, an unnamed code address, closures named by type, string data renumbered, pointers in constant data. They are now assertions the oracle runs at start. Five more are now tests: labels dropped, an unreadable point dropped, Float32 output read as a Float64 decimal, the fact's subject when f has several methods, module text not checked against its digest. One was a missing test. The fixture's counts did not change.
  - **Not done:** an input address for Enzyme's program. Its rule world is EnzymeRules methods and the optimised IR of every callee, Base's included; that is proposal **B-092**. Also not done: LLVM functions as records of their own, a relation between the two layers other than tested equivalence (they are siblings derived from f, not one lowered from the other), Mooncake, and more than one variant.

### E-7 Staged compilation (Reactant)

- **Hypothesis:** a Reactant/XLA executable is the clearest case for persisting a compiled artifact: compile time dominates, the artifact is deterministic per (shapes, types, flags, versions), and it is reused across steps and sessions.
- **Method:** compile a small Lux model's training step with Reactant; measure compile time, artifact size, determinism across runs.
- **Measures:** compile time vs step time; artifact bytes; bitwise reproducibility.
- **Fails if:** artifacts are not reproducible (then A must include a nonce, and persisting is the only way to have them again).
- **Prerequisite:** done. Reactant is installed in `experiments/ad-materialization` with its CPU build (`LocalPreferences.toml`); E-AD-0 measured it on toy functions. The GPU build would need the NVIDIA driver visible to Julia (as LenticulumLM's `.#gpu` shell does).
- **Done (B-028, 2026-11-19), Lux 1.31.6, Reactant 0.2.295 (CPU), Enzyme 0.13.216 on Julia 1.12.7.** `experiments/staged-compilation/lux_train_step.jl` ([[lux_train_step]]) compiles one training step: the MSE of a 4 → 32 → 32 → 1 MLP (1 249 `Float32` parameters, batch 64), its Enzyme gradient inside Reactant's trace, and an Adam update. It was compiled in four fresh processes (a, b, c, and p, which compiled another model first), each training 200 steps, and restored from a in a fifth. Raw output in `results.txt` there. One model, one host, one version set.
  - **Compile time dominates, but it is Julia compiling, not XLA.** Process a's timers up to the first compiled step add up to 34.9 s, without the 3.8 s of loading packages: Reactant's start-up on an unrelated function 14.3 s, the first trace of this step 18.2 s (17.9 s of it Julia compiling), the first `@compile` 1.9 s, and the first execution 0.4 s. Once Julia's code is compiled, a trace takes 0.32 s and a whole `@compile` 0.42 s. Everything `@compile` does beyond tracing, XLA included, is therefore at most about 0.1 s (89 to 112 ms in the four runs; the script's own "XLA compile ≈" line subtracts from the first `@compile` and is not that). A step then takes 47 µs at minimum and 50 to 57 µs at the median of 200, including reading the loss back to the host. A fresh process's compile costs as much as about 700 000 steps. The same step without Reactant (Enzyme on Julia arrays) takes 42 s to its first step and 25 µs per step after that. On the CPU, at this size, Reactant's step is slower than Julia's own.
  - **Restoring works and is exact.** The saved executable and glue, restored in a fresh process, train 200 steps with **bitwise identical losses and final parameters** (compared as bytes). Restoring takes 72 ms (reading files and looking up the device not included), the parameters to the device with their setup 1.6 s, and the first execution 0.65 s: 2.3 s after loading packages. A `@compile` later in that same process takes 32.4 s. It runs after the restored training, so it is not a cold measurement.
  - **Sizes.** StableHLO 17.9 KB, XLA's optimised HLO 42.9 KB, executable 121.2 KB (119.2 KB for batch 128, a separate artifact), Julia glue 19.5 KB (20.5 KB as saved, with tag, argument types and versions). The parameters are 5 KB.
  - **Reproducibility: the program is, the executable's bytes are not.** Four things are identical in all four compiling processes, the one with another history included: the StableHLO text, the HLO text, the glue's body and the whole trajectory. Each of the StableHLO's 197 lines with a location has `unknown` there, so no source paths. The saved thunk file is not identical: its tag, `reactant#1194` against `#1259` in p, is a counter of the session. The executable's bytes differ every time, even between two compiles in one process (8 of 8 distinct). Its 33 embedded ELF objects are the same set, and only their order differs (XLA's CPU backend compiles them in parallel). After sorting them (`executable_objects.py`, [[executable_objects]]), the first compiles in a, b and c are byte-identical. The other compiles differ from them in 59 bytes, all of which hold one number, XLA's module id: 2 for the session's first compile of the step, 4 after one earlier compile, 6 after two. Codex checked the sorted entries and the 59 bytes against Reactant's protobuf schema. The script itself reads the format without the schema.
  - **So the hypothesis holds for the program and not for the executable's bytes, for this step.** The StableHLO and HLO came out the same in every process. If that holds in general, per (shapes, types, flags, versions), the program's id can key the executable. That was not tested: nothing was compiled from saved text, and the compile options are not part of the text. No nonce is needed in the key. But the executable's bytes cannot be checked by recompiling and comparing unless Sophia owns a canonical form (object order, module id), and that form depends on the version. The proposal is to keep the executable as a cache entry under the program's id and the environment (Julia, Reactant, Reactant_jll, host), not as an identity: **B-093**. Reviewed by Codex ([[Review 2026-11-19 Codex B-028 (external)]]): nine findings, all right. One changed a check (the restore is now compared as bytes, re-run), and the rest changed the script's guards and this text.
  - **Not done:** compiling a saved StableHLO module in a new process (it needs Reactant's compile options by hand), a GPU, another host or model, and Lux's own `Training.single_train_step!`.

### E-10 Mathlib: statements, proofs and their checker

- **Hypothesis:** with statements as identity (as for Metamath), Mathlib's repeated statements collapse to one identity with several proofs; proof terms, 71 % of the graph, can be stored as evidence apart from statements.
- **Method:** inspect the 16 423 repeated-statement groups; measure α-equivalence (binder names erased); per theorem, constants used by the statement vs by the proof; re-check a part (then all) with `lean4checker` to turn "checked on CI" into "checked here".
- **Measures:** repeat groups by cause (aliases, generated lemmas, true duplicates); statement-vs-proof dependency counts; re-check time.
- **Fails if:** most repeats are artefacts of the export rather than real repeated statements; or re-checking is impractical (then the trust record says so).
- **Teaches:** whether the Metamath design scales to a dependently typed library; the cost of turning trusted evidence into checked evidence.
- **Measured (2026-11-20, B-029):** [[Lean Overview]] §E-10. The export already identifies statements up to binder names *and binder annotations* (lean4export shares terms by `Expr.eqv`), so the 16 423 groups were α-classes; renaming universe parameters by position gives 17 372 groups (55 182 repeated theorems, 10.6 %). Of the repeats, 88.4 % are generated by Lean (`simp`'s `_simp_N` copies, `_proof_N`), 9.0 % aliases, 2.6 % duplicates between hand-named theorems (455 groups with independent proofs). The failure condition did not occur: the repeats are real kernel statements, not export artefacts. A statement mentions 14 constants (median), its proof 19; 35 330 definitions (19 %) are reached only from proofs; no theorem rests on `sorryAx`. **Re-check:** `leanchecker` (Lean's kernel) passes 8 531 of 8 650 modules in 77.7 min (6 processes) and rejects none; 119 crash in the checker's import and are re-checked with `--fresh` (all 119 pass). Open: which of binder names, annotations and universe names belong to a statement's id ([[RQ-005 What a Lean Statement's Identity Includes]]); an independent kernel on the whole export.
